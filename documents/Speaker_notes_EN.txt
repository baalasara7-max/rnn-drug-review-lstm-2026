SLIDE 1
Lab project for Advanced NLP. We predict negative, neutral or positive sentiment from English patient reviews and compare three manually implemented LSTM cells: Vanilla, CIFG and WMC.

SLIDE 2
Ratings 1-4 are negative, 5-6 neutral and 7-10 positive. These thresholds are a modeling choice and provide proxy sentiment labels. The model receives review text only.

SLIDE 3
Raw training set: 40,075 negative, 14,356 neutral and 106,866 positive reviews. The test file has 295 missing condition values but no missing review or rating values; missing conditions do not affect this text-only task.

SLIDE 4
Exact deduplication does not eliminate near-duplicates. Subsampling preserves category proportions for the CPU training budget. Test preparation also excluded 15 conflicting rows.

SLIDE 5
Tokenization preserves internal apostrophes and separates punctuation. The vocabulary fits the full 95,443-review training pool; optimization later uses the fixed 12,000-review subset.

SLIDE 6
Sigmoid gates lie between 0 and 1. The tanh candidate g proposes new memory content. The symbol ⊙ denotes elementwise multiplication in the memory update.

SLIDE 7
CIFG sets i = 1 − f. WMC adds tanh-transformed full-matrix memory connections to the input and forget gates from c_previous; its output gate reads the newly updated c, following the appendix order. Four tests verify the explicit equations, numerical gradients, finite backward gradients, PAD invariance and parameter counts.

SLIDE 8
Class weights are computed on training data only. Environment: Python 3.12.14, PyTorch 2.14.1+cpu, four PyTorch threads, default initialization fixed by seed. Architecture selection accounts for differing parameter counts. All other representation and evaluation settings are shared.

SLIDE 9
Highlighted cell: the best validation macro-F1 overall (CIFG, 0.5246). Each run keeps the epoch with the highest validation macro-F1, and each architecture then selects its best configuration. Macro-F1 gives the three categories equal importance, whereas accuracy counts all reviews equally.

SLIDE 10
Test results did not influence configuration selection. The majority baseline reveals whether the models recognize minority categories beyond class frequency. CIFG reaches 63.78% accuracy and 0.5093 macro-F1, the strongest observed test macro-F1.

SLIDE 11
Confusion matrix of the validation-selected CIFG model; rows are actual categories and columns are predictions. Mixed sentiment, rating ambiguity and truncated context are plausible explanations to investigate, not proven causes of every error. Prediction CSVs allow individual error inspection in the same order as test_experience.tsv.

SLIDE 12
The experiment has one random seed, four epochs, a restricted optimization subset and no confidence intervals. Exact deduplication does not guarantee independence of near-duplicates or of reviews of the same drug. All final scores come from stage 10 of the repository.
